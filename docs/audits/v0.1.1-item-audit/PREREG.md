# Item audit for v0.1.1: pre-registration

Written and committed before any sample, judge answer or verdict exists.

## Purpose

Screen the flagged relation and group items of the v0.1.1 analysis for label errors. A flagged item whose gold label is wrong
or ambiguous looks like a model failure, so the release needs to know how many flagged items look mislabelled.

This audit never changes a score. It changes no label, no leaderboard entry and no flag. `flagged.jsonl` is an input to the
audit, not an output of it.

## Population

The 7000 items of the analysis: 6000 `decide_relation` items and 1000 curated `decide_group` items. The 184 reading groups
are not items. Of the 7000, 2962 are flagged and 4038 are not.

## Flag rules (`release_analysis.py`, `_item_audit`)

The halves are the 7 models with the highest MCC for the item's op (strong), and the 7 with the lowest (weak). Rank 8 is in
neither half. Each op has its own halves.

- `all_wrong`: all 15 models are wrong on the item (`n_correct` = 0).
- `inverted`: within each cell (op, gold class, stratum), each model's correctness is centred on its cell mean (a residual).
  The weak half's mean residual is above the strong half's (`residual_weak_minus_strong` > 0), and the point-biserial
  correlation `r_pb` between the item's residuals and the op's MCC over the 15 models is below 0.

An item can carry both flags. Counts in the v0.1.1 analysis: 423 `all_wrong` (87 only `all_wrong`, 336 both), 2875 `inverted`
(2539 only `inverted`, 336 both), 2962 flagged in total.

## Sample rule

Three groups, mutually exclusive, 600 items in all:

1. `all_wrong`: every one of the 423 `all_wrong` items.
2. `inverted`: 127 items flagged `inverted` and not `all_wrong`, drawn at random.
3. `control`: 50 unflagged items, drawn at random.

Stratification. A cell is (op, gold class, stratum). The gold class is the `gold` field of `flagged.jsonl` (for a relation, the
relation label; for a group, the class of its terms, such as `exactly_one` or `at_least_k:1+at_most_k:5`). The strata are the
suite strata: relations `real`, `hard_distractor`, `trivial`; groups `real`, `decoy`.

Allocation for groups 2 and 3, with N_c the population of cell c and k the number to draw: the quota is k * N_c / M, where M is
the population of the group (2539 for group 2, 4038 for group 3). Take the floor of each quota. Give the seats left over to
the cells with the largest fractional parts, ties broken by ascending cell key. Each cell's count is then within one of its
quota. Group 2 draws from the inverted items, group 3 from the unflagged items, each proportional to its own population.

Within a cell, the draw is a simple random sample without replacement from the candidates sorted by qid.

Randomness: one generator, `random.Random("item-audit-v0.1.1")`. It draws group 2's cells in ascending key order, then group
3's cells in ascending key order. Then it shuffles the 600 rows, and the shuffled rows are numbered A001 to A600.

Why controls follow the unflagged population. The control's purpose is the judges' false-alarm rate on items that the analysis
did not flag. Those items are drawn in the same cells, in proportion to their own counts.

## Prompts

Each item's prompt is the exact child wire text: the state with the league rule card of each outcome asked about, the
question's instructions and its options. A group prompt adds the neutral instruction for the kind, bounds and chain. The
prompts are captured through the real child, against an in-process fake server (`build_sample.py`). No model server is
contacted.

`sample.jsonl` holds `id`, `type`, `prompt` and `answer_format` only. It holds no gold, qid, flag, stratum or group.

## Judges

- Two independent judge agents, each a new Haiku 5.5 instance (claude-haiku-5-5). Each judge works in its own empty folder
  holding only `JUDGE.md` and `sample.jsonl`, and writes `judgeN_answers.jsonl` there. The judges are blind to the gold, the
  key, the flags, the qids, each other's answers and the verdict rule.
- `JUDGE.md` is the frozen `docs/audits/test-set-relations/JUDGE.md`, with the binding and reading items removed. Nothing else
  changes.
- The main agent copies the two answer files back into this folder as `judge1_answers.jsonl` and `judge2_answers.jsonl`.
- Amendment (2026-11-01, committed before any judging): `sample.jsonl` (600 items, 1.4 MB) exceeds one Haiku instance's
  context, as the frozen audit's judge 2 did at 210 items. Each judge is therefore a SET of six fresh Haiku 5.5 instances,
  one per consecutive slice of 100 rows of the unchanged `sample.jsonl` (A001-A100, A101-A200, ..., A501-A600). Each instance
  sees only `JUDGE.md` and its slice, in its own empty folder, and is blind to the other slices, the other judge set, the
  gold, the key, the flags and the verdict rule. Judge 1's six slice answers are concatenated in id order into
  `judge1_answers.jsonl`; judge 2's likewise. A slice whose answer file is missing ids or malformed is re-run once by a fresh
  instance; ids still missing count as no answer (the item is then `unclear`).
- Reporting note: the 50 controls hold only 5 group items, so the false-alarm rate is mostly a relation estimate; any report
  states this.

Normalisation of an answer:

- Relation: the label, one of EQUIV, IMPLIES, IMPLIED_BY, EXCLUDES, NONE.
- Group: kind `none` gives the class `none`. Otherwise the class is `terms_class(group_terms(lower, upper, n, kind ==
  ordered_chain))`, where n is the group's size. A kind or bound that the canonical terms cannot hold is invalid. The chain is
  collected and not scored.
- An invalid or missing answer normalises to null.

## Verdict rule

For each item, with the gold class from the key and the two normalised answers:

- `hard_valid`: both judges give the gold class.
- `suspect_label`: both judges give the same answer, and it is not the gold class.
- `unclear`: all other items. This covers disagreement between the judges, one judge matching gold and the other not, a
  null answer, and two different non-gold answers.

`suspect_label` marks an item for review. It does not show the gold is wrong.

## Controls and false-alarm rate

Controls are reported separately. The false-alarm rate is the share of the 50 controls labelled `suspect_label`. The
suspect share of the flagged items (the `all_wrong` and `inverted` samples) is compared with that rate. The report gives sample
counts. No population estimate is pre-registered.

## No adjudication

No person adjudicates in this release. Per-item verdicts, judge answers and counts are published. Items labelled
`suspect_label` are not removed or relabelled here.

## Outputs

`score.py` writes `verdicts.json` (schema `mentat-bench-item-verdicts/1`) in this folder, with `sample_rule`, `n_judged`,
`n_flagged` (2962), `sampled: true`, `counts` (the three verdicts for the flagged items sampled), `controls` (`n`, the three
verdicts and `false_alarm_rate`), `by_group`, and `items` (qid, op, gold, group, judge1, judge2, verdict), sorted by qid. The
output is deterministic. `release_analysis.py` reads `counts` as `item_audit.judged`.

This audit changes no score, label, flag or file outside `docs/audits/v0.1.1-item-audit/`.
